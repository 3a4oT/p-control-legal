01 What p-control is

p-control runs on an Android TV device and helps a parent enforce app time limits and block lists on that device. A parent manages the household's rules through a companion Telegram bot, connected to the TV app over an encrypted MQTT link to a server the developer operates. There is no separate mobile app or web login — the bot is the entire admin surface.

What is being built next: a web admin panel, served by the same household service, where a parent signs in from a browser instead of managing everything through chat. It is not available yet. When it ships it adds a browser session for the parent — a sign-in confirmed through Telegram, and a session cookie — and its requests reach the service through the hosting providers named in §5. It does not change what the television collects or sends. This page will be updated, with a new effective date, before that panel is available to anyone.

02 Data collected on the TV

Everything below stays on the device except where this page says otherwise.

App usage (for enforcement)

p-control reads which app is in the foreground via Android's Usage Access API to detect and block restricted apps, and to compute daily screen time. This runs continuously while the app is active, and the reading itself never leaves the TV: the current app version transmits no usage history, no screen-time totals and no installed-app list at all.

Three things do leave the TV, and only these: the identifiers below when a parent pairs the device; the identifier of one specific app at the moment a child uses "ask a parent" to request it (so the parent knows what they are approving); and a short acknowledgement that a settings update was received.

Device identifiers sent when pairing

Pairing a TV to a household sends the household's server:

  • a random identifier p-control generates for this installation;
  • a device identifier Android provides to this app. It is specific to p-control and to the device's user profile — it cannot be used to recognise this television in any other app, and it is not an advertising identifier. It is sent for one purpose: so that reinstalling p-control is recognised as the same television returning, instead of leaving the old device stranded in the parent's list;
  • the device model reported by Android (e.g. "Xiaomi MiBox4"), so a parent with several televisions can tell them apart when confirming the pairing.

An unpaired TV sends none of this. Nothing is transmitted before a parent confirms the pairing in Telegram.

Product analytics

p-control uses Firebase Analytics to record anonymous, count-only product events — for example gate_shown, app_blocked, pairing_succeeded. These events carry no names, no pairing codes, no device identifiers, and no information about which specific apps were blocked.

Crash reports

p-control uses Firebase Crashlytics to record crash and error reports if something goes wrong. Reports contain a stack trace and a short technical label describing what the app was doing (e.g. enforcement_loop_crash) — never a profile name, PIN, pairing code, or device identifier.

Not collected: advertising identifiers, location, contacts, microphone, or camera. p-control does not show ads and does not use an ads SDK.

05 Who your data is shared with

  • The household's own parent, via the Telegram bot — that is the product's purpose.
  • Google Firebase (Analytics, Crashlytics) — as a data processor, for the anonymous events and crash reports described above.
  • Telegram — the bot's messages are delivered through Telegram's own infrastructure, subject to Telegram's own privacy terms.
  • Hetzner — the household service and its database run on servers Hetzner operates, in the EU. Hetzner is a hosting provider acting on the developer's instructions; it does not use the data for its own purposes.
  • Cloudflare — the service's web endpoints are reached through Cloudflare, which therefore handles the connecting IP address and request metadata. The television's own link to the service does not pass through it.
  • No data is sold. No data is shared with advertisers or data brokers.

08 Children's data

p-control is a tool a parent installs and configures — a child does not create an account, does not interact with Telegram, and does not provide any data directly. Everything in §3 about a child (profile name, usage) is entered or generated by the household's own device, on the parent's instruction, for the parent's own use in managing that household.

Two consequences of that design are stated here rather than glossed over: a child's own requests about their data are reachable only through the parent's account, and the service has no separate handling for teenagers. Whether that arrangement is sufficient where you live depends on your jurisdiction's rules for children's data.

09 Access, correction, deletion

A parent can remove any child profile, app rule, or the pairing itself at any time from the Telegram bot. Unpairing a device clears its stored credentials. To request full deletion of a household's data from the server, contact us (§12).

Where the law gives them to you, you also have the right to ask for a copy of your data, to have it corrected, to have processing restricted, to object to processing we base on legitimate interests, and to receive your data in a portable form. Ask through §12; we answer within one month.

Where your data goes. The household service and its database run in the EU. Firebase (crash reports and count-only analytics) is operated by Google and may process data outside your country under Google's own safeguards; Telegram delivers the bot's messages under its own terms. If you are in the UK or EU/EEA and believe we have handled your data wrongly, you may complain to your national data-protection authority.

10 Security measures

  • The TV app never stores its pairing/MQTT credentials in plain text — they're encrypted at rest using Android Keystore-backed encryption (Google Tink).
  • The block/stop screen and PIN entry screens are excluded from screenshots and the recent-apps preview (FLAG_SECURE).
  • The TV app talks to the server exclusively over an encrypted MQTT connection (TLS).
  • A parent-set PIN gates access to the app's own settings on the TV.
