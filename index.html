01 Who we are

p-control is published by Petro Rovenskyi, an individual developer, who is the data controller for everything described on this page. There is no company behind it and no third-party operator.

Privacy contact: petro.rovenskyi@gmail.com. Every request under §11 goes to that address and is answered by a person.

02 What p-control is

p-control runs on an Android TV and helps a parent block apps and enforce time limits on that television. A parent manages the rules through a companion Telegram bot, which talks to the TV over an encrypted connection to a server we operate. There is no separate mobile app and no account to create.

Coming, not yet available: a web admin panel served by the same service, where a parent signs in from a browser instead of chatting with the bot. It will add a browser session — a sign-in confirmed through Telegram, and a session cookie — and will not change what the television collects or sends. This page will be updated, with a new effective date, before it is available to anyone.

03 What stays on the television

p-control reads which app is in the foreground, using Android’s Usage Access permission, so it can tell whether that app should be blocked and how long it has been open. This reading never leaves the television. We do not send, and the app contains no code that could send, your usage history, your screen-time totals, or the list of apps installed on the device.

The parent’s PIN is stored only as a salted hash, never as the PIN itself.

We never collect what you type, what is on your screen, screenshots, your clipboard, the addresses of pages you visit, file names, your location, your contacts, your microphone or your camera. p-control shows no ads and contains no advertising SDK. That list is a design rule for us, not a statement about this version only.

04 What leaves the television

An unpaired television sends nothing at all. Once a parent confirms the pairing in Telegram, exactly three things travel:

  1. At pairing: a random identifier we generate for this installation; a device identifier Android provides to this app, which is specific to p-control and to this device’s user profile, cannot be used to recognise the television in any other app, and is not an advertising identifier; and the device model reported by Android (e.g. “Xiaomi MiBox4”). The device identifier exists for one purpose — so that reinstalling p-control is recognised as the same television returning, instead of leaving a dead entry in the parent’s device list.
  2. When a child asks a parent to unblock something: the identifier of that one app, so the parent knows what they are being asked to approve.
  3. After the parent changes a rule: a short acknowledgement that the television received it.

07 Who else sees it

We do not sell data, and we share it with no advertiser or data broker. These parties are involved:

  • The household’s own parent, through the Telegram bot. That is the product’s purpose.
  • Google (Firebase) — crash reports and anonymous, count-only product events such as app_blocked. These carry no names, no codes, no profile information and nothing about which specific app was blocked.
  • Telegram — delivers the bot’s messages, under its own terms.
  • Hetzner — hosts the service and its database in the EU, on our instructions only.
  • Cloudflare — sits in front of the service’s web endpoints and therefore handles connecting IP addresses and request metadata. The television’s own link does not pass through it.

08 Where it goes

The service and its database are in the EU. Google (Firebase), Telegram and Cloudflare may process data outside the EEA under their own transfer safeguards, including the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses where those apply. We add no further destinations of our own.

10 Children, and your duty to tell them

p-control is installed and configured by a parent. A child creates no account, never interacts with Telegram, and gives us nothing directly. Everything in §5 concerning a child is entered by the parent, or generated by the household’s own television on the parent’s instruction.

Monitoring software should not be a secret from the person being monitored. By using p-control you confirm that you hold parental responsibility for the household, and that you have told anyone old enough to understand it that this television runs control and monitoring software.

Two consequences of this design are stated rather than glossed over: a child’s own requests about their data reach us only through the parent’s account, and there is no separate handling for teenagers. Whether that is sufficient depends on where you live — the rules differ between COPPA in the United States and national implementations of the GDPR in Europe.

11 Your rights

Some of these you can exercise yourself, immediately, without asking us:

  • Remove a child profile, a rule, or the pairing itself — from the Telegram bot, at any time.
  • Uninstall the app — which ends all collection on that television.

For the rest, write to petro.rovenskyi@gmail.com:

  • Access — a copy of what we hold about you.
  • Correction — fix anything inaccurate.
  • Erasure — delete a household and everything in §5.
  • Restriction — have us stop using data while a dispute is resolved.
  • Objection — to anything we base on legitimate interests (§6).
  • Portability — your data in a machine-readable form.

We reply within one month. If a request is unusually complex we may take a further two months and will tell you why within the first. Exercising any of these costs nothing and changes nothing about how the app works for you.

If you are in the UK or EU/EEA and think we have got this wrong, you may complain to your national data-protection authority.

12 How we protect it

  • The television’s credentials are encrypted at rest with a key held in Android’s hardware-backed Keystore.
  • The parent’s PIN is stored as a salted hash, never as the PIN.
  • The block screen and the PIN screens are excluded from screenshots and from the recent-apps preview.
  • The television talks to the service only over an encrypted (TLS) connection, and each television is issued its own credential that reaches only its own data.
  • The service and database run in the EU with access restricted to the developer.

No system is perfect, and we will not pretend otherwise. If we ever discover a breach affecting your data, we will tell affected households and the relevant authority as the law requires.
